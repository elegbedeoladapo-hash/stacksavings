## Interview 1 — Kunle, Startup Team Member

**Role:** Startup team member (tech team)
**Company stage:** Early-stage startup
**Date:** 2026-05-21

**Question 1: What AI tools does your team spend money on right now?**

"Right now, our group covers costs for ChatGPT Plus, along with GitHub Copilot and Notion AI. At one point, we tested out Midjourney just a short run for certain visual tasks."

**Question 2: What's your startup's monthly AI tool cost, about?**

"Usually lands around $150 to $250 each month. Team size shifts that number some. Costs climb quick without warning.

**Question 3: Maybe your AI tools aren't pulling their weight. Some could be sitting idle while you still pay. Worth questioning if each one earns its spot.**

"Truth is, some of the AI tools we pay for sit idle more than expected. While ChatGPT Plus stays busy every day, Notion AI tends to fade into the background easily forgotten. Developers lean on Copilot often, yet others outside tech rarely open it at all.

**Question 4: Was there a time you stopped using an AI service due to price?**

Here's what played out. We walked away from Jasper AI within eight weeks. The pitch said it would handle writing tasks, yet ChatGPT delivered sharper results. Since we had access to that already, keeping both made little sense. Suddenly one bill felt like wasted space on the monthly list.

**Question 5: A free tool pops up. It points straight at your AI spending leaks. Shows where cash drains out. Offers less costly options instead. Would that change how you handle things now?**

"For sure. Knowing exactly where money goes and what tools get used would make things a lot clearer. The real issue sits in how subscriptions spread out charged on individual team members' cards, leaving gaps in understanding.

**Most surprising thing they said:**
Subscriptions spread across individual team members' cards meaning no single person has a complete picture of total AI spend. This is a bigger problem than just overspending on one tool.

**What it changed about the design:**
This confirmed that the team size field in the form is critical the spending problem is partly an organizational visibility problem, not just a pricing problem. StackSavings gives the first consolidated view many teams have ever seen.

## Interview 2 — Anonymous, Frontend Developer (LinkedIn)

**Role:** Frontend Developer
**Company stage:** Software development team
**Date:** 2026-05-21

**Question 1: What AI tools does your team spend money on right now?**

"Certainly. Cursor Pro along with ChatGPT Plus. Nothing more."

**Question 2: What's your startup's monthly cost for AI tools, about?**

"Around forty dollars each month for my own use. For everyone on the team, perhaps two hundred."

**Question 3: Maybe your AI tools aren't pulling their weight. Some could be sitting idle while you still pay. Worth questioning if each one earns its spot.**

"Worth every dollar? Cursor definitely. As for ChatGPT Plus half the days it feels necessary, half the days not. Truth is, the basic version might cover what I actually need."

**Question 4: One time, prices added up so fast it felt like smoke - what happened when you saw the bill?**

"Out of nowhere, I switched from GitHub Copilot to Cursor no looking back. Right away, one thing stood out: it doesn't just guess, it gets what I'm building. While Copilot hands me fragments, Cursor pieces together context like it's been reading every file. First real helper that doesn't need hand-holding. The shift happened fast - one test run and everything else faded. Not magic, just finally built right."

**Question 5: Should such a resource point out costly mistakes in your AI spending while offering budget options, does that sound useful?**

"For sure, I'd give it a go assuming there's no hidden catch behind the 'free' label. Teams building software tend to ditch anything that drains resources without reason."

**Most surprising thing they said:**
ChatGPT Plus feels unnecessary half the time they suspected the free version might cover their actual needs. This is exactly the kind of insight StackSavings surfaces.

**What it changed about the design:**
The "free" skepticism is real. This confirmed that the "No login required, free forever" messaging on the landing page needs to be prominent and credible users are conditioned to distrust free tools.

## Interview 3 — Boluwatife, Product Designer (LinkedIn)

**Role:** Product Designer
**Company stage:** Design/product team
**Date:** 2026-05-21

**Question 1: What AI tools does your team spend money on right now?**

"For now, only Midjourney plus Claude Pro are in play. I resisted adding extras because clutter hampers how you really work."

**Question 2: What's your startup's monthly AI tool spending look like, about?**

"Truth is, I can't name the precise count for everyone on the team - tracking just isn't happening the way it should, and that gap? That's where things fall apart."

**Question 3: Ever wonder if every dollar spent on AI actually pays off, yet a few just sit there unused?**

"Most days I turn to Claude when drafting briefs, digging into user feedback, or reviewing designs. Yet Midjourney? It sits idle for weeks until suddenly I'm deep in it for some urgent task. Honestly, paying every month feels harder to defend now."

**Question 4: Ever stopped using an AI service after realizing the price didn't match what you got?**

"Three months in, we stopped using Galileo AI. Flashy demos aside, the actual UI outputs demanded endless fixes - building manually became quicker than editing its results. What we got didn't feel like a finished product, more like funding someone else's beta test."

**Question 5: Imagine a no-cost helper pointing straight at your AI spending leaks, then whispering smarter, lighter options. Would that change how you move?**

"Shouldn't have to link every account just to use it. That kind of access makes me uneasy. Still, the core thought? Absolutely necessary. Right now, zero clarity on what our group spends overall."

**Most surprising thing they said:**
"Zero clarity on what our group spends overall" — a product designer at a functioning team has no idea what their total AI spend is. This isn't a startup problem, it's a visibility problem that affects every team regardless of size.

**What it changed about the design:**
Boluwatife's concern about "linking every account" confirmed that StackSavings must never ask for API keys or account access — just self-reported spend numbers. The manual input approach is the right call. Privacy and simplicity over accuracy.
